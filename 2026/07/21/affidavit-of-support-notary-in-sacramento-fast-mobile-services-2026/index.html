What is an Affidavit of Support and Why Does it Require a Notary?

An Affidavit of Support is a formal, legally binding contract between an individual sponsor and the U.S. Government. By signing this document, you’re promising to provide financial support to an intending immigrant so they don’t become a public charge. Because of the serious financial obligations involved, the government needs to ensure that the person signing the form is exactly who they claim to be. This is where an affidavit of support notary Sacramento professional becomes essential.

Understanding what is an affidavit helps clarify why these documents require such strict verification. The notary acts as an impartial witness. Their primary job is to verify your identity and confirm that you’re signing the document of your own free will. This verification process is a critical safeguard against fraudulent execution. When a notary applies their official seal, it signals to USCIS that the sponsor’s signature is authentic and that the document was executed under penalty of perjury. Without this layer of security, the risk of document rejection increases significantly, potentially delaying the entire immigration process for your loved ones.

While some versions of USCIS forms might not explicitly state that a notary is mandatory for every single page, legal advisors almost always recommend it. Having a formal notarization adds a layer of undeniable validity to the document. It shows that you’ve taken the necessary steps to formalize your commitment. If you’re working with an affidavit of support notary Sacramento specialist, they ensure that the specific California requirements for notarization are met perfectly, leaving no room for USCIS to question the document’s legitimacy.

Common Forms: I-864, I-134, and Beyond

Most sponsors in Sacramento deal with either Form I-864 or Form I-134. The I-864 is typically used for family-based or some employment-based immigrant visas. In contrast, the I-134 is generally for non-immigrant visas, such as those for temporary visitors or fiancé(e)s. Both forms carry high stakes because they involve your financial liability. It’s important to remember that a notary is not an attorney. We can witness your signature and verify your identity, but we cannot provide legal advice on which form you should use or how to fill out specific fields.

The Legal Significance of the Jurat in California

In California, an Affidavit of Support usually requires a specific notarial act called a Jurat. Unlike a simple acknowledgment, which just confirms you signed the document, a Jurat requires you to take an oath or affirmation. You must swear that the information within the document is true and correct to the best of your knowledge. California Government Code Section 8202 defines the Jurat as the act where a notary certifies that the signer took an oath and signed the document in their presence. This higher level of verification is specifically designed for documents where the truth of the statements is paramount.

California Notary Requirements for Affidavits in 2026

California law is very specific about how a notary must verify your identity. In 2026, the foundational requirement remains the personal appearance of the signer. This means you must be physically present in the same room as the notary at the time of signing. Since California has not yet fully implemented Remote Online Notarization, you cannot use a video call for this process. You must meet your notary in person to complete the verification.

When you meet with an affidavit of support notary Sacramento specialist, you’ll need to provide valid, government-issued identification. The ID must contain a photograph, a description of the person, a signature, and a serial number. Acceptable forms of ID in Sacramento include:

  • A current California Driver’s License or ID card.
  • A U.S. Passport or Passport Card.
  • A foreign passport that has been stamped by the U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services (USCIS) or U.S. Customs and Border Protection.
  • A valid driver’s license issued by another U.S. state.
  • A California inmate identification card, if the signer is in custody.

Identity Verification Standards

If your identification is expired, California law allows for its use only if it was issued within the last five years. If you don’t have a valid ID that meets these criteria, you might need to use credible witnesses. This involves two people who personally know you, have valid IDs themselves, and can swear under oath to your identity. It’s a more complex process, so if you’re unsure about your ID’s status, it’s helpful to schedule a mobile notary appointment in advance to discuss your specific situation. Ensure that the name on your ID matches the name typed on your Form I-864 exactly. Even a missing middle initial can cause issues during the verification process.

The Notary Journal and Thumbprint Requirement

California notaries must maintain a sequential journal of all notarial acts. For every signature, the notary records the date, time, type of act, and the method of identification used. While California law specifically mandates a right thumbprint for documents involving real property or powers of attorney, it’s not strictly required for an Affidavit of Support. However, many professional notaries still request a thumbprint as an extra security measure. This practice protects the sponsor by creating a definitive record that they were the one who appeared and signed. It’s a standard part of the protocol for a professional affidavit of support notary Sacramento service and serves as a powerful deterrent against identity theft.

How to Prepare for Your Affidavit Notarization Appointment

Proper preparation ensures your appointment with an affidavit of support notary Sacramento professional is efficient and successful. When you organize your documents ahead of time, the notarization process remains a quick and stress-free part of your day. Following a specific sequence prevents common errors that could lead to document rejection by USCIS.

  • Step 1: Complete the form entirely. Use the most current version of Form I-864, which has an edition date of October 17, 2024. Fill in every required field, but do not sign or date the signature line.
  • Step 2: Prepare your original ID. You must present a physical, government-issued ID card. Digital copies or paper photocopies are not acceptable for identity verification under California law.
  • Step 3: Check for name consistency. Verify that the name typed on the Affidavit of Support matches the name on your identification exactly. If your ID includes a middle name that is missing from the form, update the document before the notary arrives.
  • Step 4: Set up a workspace. Arrange a quiet, well-lit area with a flat surface, such as a dining table or desk. This allows the notary to easily manage their journal and apply the official seal.
  • Step 5: Coordinate witnesses. If your specific form or legal counsel specifies the need for additional witnesses, ensure they are present and have their own valid identification ready.

Common Mistakes That Delay Notarization

What is the most frequent error sponsors make? Signing the document before the notary arrives is the number one mistake. If you sign the form early, the notary cannot certify that they witnessed the act of signing. If this happens, you will need to provide a fresh, unsigned copy of the form. Another common delay occurs when sponsors provide an incomplete document. Ensure every page of the affidavit is present, as notaries cannot notarize a document that appears incomplete or has missing pages.

Witness Requirements for Immigration Forms

Sponsors often ask if they need to bring their own witnesses. For most standard Affidavit of Support forms, the notary public is the only official witness required. However, if you are signing a different type of immigration document or if your legal advisor has added a witness line, those individuals must be physically present. A witness cannot be someone with a financial interest in the document. You should always check your specific USCIS instructions to see if your particular filing requires third-party witnesses in addition to the notarial act.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need to sign the Affidavit of Support before the notary arrives?

No, you shouldn’t sign the Affidavit of Support before your notary arrives. The notary must personally witness the act of signing to certify the document. If you sign the form early, the notary will ask you to provide a fresh, unsigned copy. This ensures the affidavit of support notary Sacramento professional can legally verify that you’re the person who executed the document in their presence.

What forms of ID are acceptable for a notary in Sacramento?

Acceptable identification in Sacramento includes a current California driver’s license, a U.S. passport, or a foreign passport stamped by USCIS. The ID must be an original, government-issued document that contains your photograph, signature, and a physical description. If your ID is expired, California law only allows its use if it was issued within the last five years. Proper identification is the most critical requirement for a successful appointment.

Can a notary help me fill out my I-864 form?

No, a notary public cannot help you fill out Form I-864 or provide any legal advice. We’re prohibited by law from explaining the legal effects of the form or telling you which information to enter in the financial sections. If you’re unsure how to complete the fields, you should consult with an immigration attorney. Our role is strictly limited to verifying your identity and witnessing your signature.

How much does a mobile notary charge in Sacramento?

The total cost for a mobile notary in Sacramento consists of a state-regulated per-signature fee and a separate travel fee. While the signature fee is capped by California law, the travel fee varies based on the distance, time of day, and specific meeting location. Professional transparency is important to us, so we provide clear information regarding these logistical costs before we confirm your mobile appointment.

Can you notarize an Affidavit of Support at a hospital?

Yes, we specialize in hospital bedside notarizations for sponsors who are unable to travel due to illness or injury. We frequently visit facilities like UC Davis Medical Center and Kaiser Permanente Sacramento to assist families with urgent immigration documentation. We understand the specific protocols for medical environments and work quickly to ensure the process is as stress-free as possible for the patient and their family.

What happens if the sponsor cannot physically sign the document?

If a sponsor is physically unable to sign their name, California law allows for a “signature by mark.” This process requires two witnesses who aren’t mentioned in the document to observe the sponsor making a mark, such as an “X,” on the signature line. The notary then records this act in their journal. If the sponsor is mentally incapacitated and cannot understand the document, the notarization cannot proceed.

Do I need a witness for an Affidavit of Support in California?

For most standard USCIS Affidavit of Support forms, a third-party witness isn’t required because the notary serves as the official witness. However, you should always check the specific instructions for your filing edition, as requirements can change. If your legal advisor or the specific form instructions do require additional witnesses, they must be physically present with their own valid IDs during the affidavit of support notary Sacramento appointment.

Is a mobile notary the same as a regular notary public?

Yes, a mobile notary holds the same commission and legal authority as any other notary public in California. The only difference is that a mobile service travels to your home, office, or a hospital rather than requiring you to visit a retail store. This provides a more private and convenient setting for handling sensitive financial and immigration documents while ensuring the same level of legal verification.
