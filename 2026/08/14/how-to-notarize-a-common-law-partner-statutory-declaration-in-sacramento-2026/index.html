What is a Common Law Partner Statutory Declaration?

A Statutory declaration is a formal, written statement of fact that a person signs and declares to be true in the presence of an authorized official. For couples, this document serves as legal evidence that two individuals live together in a committed, marriage-like relationship despite not being legally married. It’s a high-stakes document because it carries the weight of an oath. Making a false statement on this form can result in serious legal consequences, including perjury.

When you meet with a common law partner statutory declaration notary, their primary function is to maintain the integrity of the signing process. They don’t validate your relationship history or the “truth” of your partnership. Instead, they focus on procedural accuracy. The notary verifies your identity using government-issued photo ID, ensures you’re signing of your own free will, and witnesses the “solemn declaration” where you swear the contents are true. This verification process is what gives the document its legal weight for government agencies and insurance providers.

Common Law vs. Domestic Partnership in California

It’s vital to clarify that California abolished common-law marriage in 1895. You can’t become “legally married” simply by living together for a specific number of years within the state. Because of this, a statutory declaration isn’t a substitute for a marriage certificate in the eyes of California state law. It differs significantly from a formal State of California Domestic Partnership registration, which involves filing Form DP-1 with the Secretary of State to gain specific state-level rights. Despite this, private companies and foreign governments frequently accept these notarized declarations as sufficient proof of a stable union for specific benefits or visa categories. They act as a bridge between your personal life and the administrative requirements of outside organizations, which is why finding a qualified common law partner statutory declaration notary is a critical first step.

When You Might Need This Notarized

While the state doesn’t recognize the union for marital rights, several entities require these documents to grant access to specific programs. Common scenarios include:

  • International Immigration: The most frequent use is for Canadian immigration via Form IMM 5409, or for similar visa applications in Australia and the United Kingdom. These countries often require a “Common-Law Union” declaration to process sponsorship.
  • Employer Benefits: Many Sacramento-based employers allow staff to add partners to their health insurance, provided they submit a notarized declaration of their shared residence and financial interdependence.
  • Legal Standing: These forms can provide a layer of evidence for medical or financial decision-making if you haven’t yet established a formal Power of Attorney. They help prove a shared life to hospital administrators or financial institutions.

Requirements for a Valid Statutory Declaration in 2026

For a common law partner statutory declaration notary to execute their duties correctly, certain logistical and legal criteria must be met before the session starts. Most importantly, both partners must be physically present at the same time. California law doesn’t allow for “piecemeal” notarization where partners sign at different times or locations. This simultaneous presence ensures the notary can witness both signatures and administer the required verbal affirmation to both parties at once.

Identification is the next hurdle. Each signer must present a valid, unexpired government-issued photo ID. Acceptable forms include a California driver’s license, a US passport, or a foreign passport that’s been stamped by USCIS. If your ID has expired within the last five years, it might still be acceptable under specific California codes, but it’s always best to have current documentation ready to prevent delays. If you’re unsure if your documents are ready for the appointment, you can schedule a mobile notary consultation to review your specific needs.

The document itself must be entirely filled out before the notary arrives. You should leave only the signature and date fields blank. During the process, you’ll be asked to perform a “solemn affirmation.” This isn’t just a formality; it’s a verbal pledge that carries the same legal weight as an oath taken in a courtroom. By making this affirmation, you’re legally stating that every piece of information in the document is true to the best of your knowledge.

Essential Information for the Form

Accuracy is paramount when filling out these declarations. You’ll need to provide the exact date your continuous cohabitation began and the specific date you consider your relationship to have started. Ensure all names are written exactly as they appear on your legal identification. While the notary doesn’t review your bank statements or utility bills, you should have these records organized for your own files, as the agency receiving your declaration may request them as supporting evidence of your shared affairs.

California Notary Certificate Compliance

Many international forms, like those from Canada or the UK, include pre-printed notary blocks that don’t meet the strict wording requirements set by the California Secretary of State. If the form’s language is non-compliant, your notary will attach a separate certificate, usually a Jurat or an Acknowledgment. This attachment is a standard legal procedure in 2026 to ensure the document is recognized as valid within the United States and abroad. Don’t be alarmed if the notary staples a separate page to your form; this is a sign of a prepared professional who is protecting your document from being rejected for technical errors.

International Use: The Apostille Requirement

An Apostille is an international certification that validates the signature, seal, and position of the official who notarized your document. If you are sending your partnership declaration to a foreign government, that agency has no way of verifying a local Sacramento notary’s credentials on their own. The California Secretary of State serves as the essential bridge in this process. They compare the notary’s commission status and signature against their database and attach a numbered certificate to your declaration. This certificate is recognized by all countries that are members of the Hague Convention of 1961.

The authentication process functions as a chain where every link must be flawless. If the common law partner statutory declaration notary makes a minor error on the certificate wording or uses an outdated 2026 California Jurat format, the Secretary of State will reject the document. This technical failure can cause significant delays in your visa or property application. In 2026, the Secretary of State continues to offer in-person services at their 11th Street office in Sacramento. This allows for rapid authentication, often within the same business day, provided you pay the state’s $20 fee and the $6 special handling charge for in-person submissions.
