18:T121e,<h2>Before You Book: What Every Sacramento Signer Should Know</h2>
<p>Most notarization delays happen for one of three reasons: an expired or mismatched photo ID, a document signed before the notary arrives (jurats require signing in the notary’s physical presence), or an incomplete certificate block. Here is a quick reference covering California notary law (<a href="https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displayText.xhtml?division=1.&amp;chapter=3.&amp;part=&amp;lawCode=GOV&amp;title=2." rel="noopener">California Government Code §§8200–8230</a>), acceptable identification, and how our mobile dispatch works across the Sacramento region.</p>
<h2>1. Identification &amp; Credible Witness Rules (California Civil Code §1185)</h2>
<p>California has some of the strictest notary identification standards in the United States. Every signer must present a government-issued photo ID that is either current or issued within the last five years and bears a serial number, photograph, physical description, and signature:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Primary acceptable IDs:</strong> California Driver’s License or California ID card, U.S. Passport or Passport Card, U.S. Military ID card, Driver’s License or official ID issued by another U.S. state, Mexican or Canadian driver’s license, or a valid foreign passport stamped by USCIS.</li>
<li><strong>When the signer has no current ID:</strong> Under <a href="https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&amp;sectionNum=1185." rel="noopener">Civil Code §1185(b)</a>, identity may be established by the oath of two credible witnesses who personally know the signer, each present their own qualifying government photo ID, and have no financial interest in or named role on the document being signed.</li>
<li><strong>Name matching:</strong> The name on your ID must cover the name printed on the document (you may have more names on your ID than on the document, such as a full middle name on the ID and a middle initial on the document, but not the reverse).</li>
</ul>
<h2>2. Pricing &amp; Statutory Fees (California Government Code §8211)</h2>
<p>California sets the notary fee at <strong>$15 per notarized signature</strong> for acknowledgments and jurats under <a href="https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=GOV&amp;sectionNum=8211." rel="noopener">Government Code §8211</a>. As a mobile notary service, Sacramento Notary Co charges a visit fee that covers travel and scheduling, plus the $15 per signature, shown separately and quoted before we leave:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Standard visit (8 AM–8 PM, <a href="/same-day-notary-sacramento/">same-day included</a>):</strong> $55 visit fee · $70 with one signature</li>
<li><strong>Priority (we leave within the hour):</strong> $80 visit fee · $95 with one signature</li>
<li><strong>Evening (8–10 PM):</strong> $95 visit fee · $110 with one signature; overnight (10 PM–8 AM) by request, $150 visit fee</li>
<li><strong><a href="/hospital-bedside/">Hospital</a>, <a href="/hospice-notary/">hospice, skilled nursing, assisted living</a>:</strong> $150 with up to two signatures · $15 each additional</li>
<li><strong><a href="/loan-signing-agent-sacramento/">Loan Signing</a>:</strong> flat packages: Seller $200 · HELOC $200 · Refinance $250 · Purchase $250</li>
<li><strong><a href="/wills-trusts-notary/">Estate signing</a> (trust, pour-over will, <a href="/power-of-attorney-notary-sacramento/">POA</a>, healthcare directive):</strong> visit fee + $15 per signature, capped at $175 for up to two signers at home; bedside estate signings use the $150 bedside rate</li>
<li><strong><a href="/apostille-sacramento/">California Secretary of State Apostille</a>:</strong> $150 standard (5–10 business days) or $199 same-day counter filing, all state fees included; each additional document $60</li>
</ul>
